Seven Stages of Rebuilding

From your first night with nothing to a functioning industrial society. Each stage builds on the last.

Stage 1: Day Zero

First 72 hours

Water procurement, emergency shelter, fire from friction, threat assessment, psychological stabilization, immediate first aid.

Stage 2: Primitive Survival

First month

Hunting and trapping, plant identification, hide processing, cordage, flintknapping, food preservation, sanitation systems.

Stage 3: Settlement

First year

Permanent shelter construction, agriculture, animal husbandry, pottery, basic metallurgy, community water systems.

Stage 4: Village Economy

Years 2–5

Blacksmithing, textiles, carpentry, masonry, trade systems, food surplus management, basic governance.

Stage 5: Organized Society

Years 5–20

Education systems, advanced medicine, engineering, road building, legal frameworks, currency, specialized trades.

Stage 6: Pre-Industrial

Years 20–50

Water power, advanced metallurgy, chemistry, optics, printing, navigation, large-scale agriculture.

Stage 7: Industrial Revolution

Years 50–100

Steam power, electricity, telecommunications, precision manufacturing, modern medicine, industrial chemistry.

What It Reads Like

From the chapter on Bloomery Iron — turning rocks into metal with mud and charcoal:

Iron is the metal that matters. Copper is useful. Bronze is better. But iron is what separates a community that struggles from one that thrives. It's the metal that makes real tools, real weapons, real infrastructure possible. Every other metal you'll work with is either a stepping stone toward iron or a specialty material for specific applications.

This is the bottleneck skill. Everything downstream of it — steel, machinery, construction hardware, agricultural implements — runs faster once you're through it. Get this wrong and you'll produce a worthless lump of glassy slag and heartbreak. Get it right and you've unlocked the single most transformative capability in pre-industrial technology.
